Proposal & product strategy · Oct 2026

Self-service configuration: a proposal for business-owned setup

I built an evidence-based case that routine configuration should not ride the code release train, and proposed a business-owned console for program setup, state compliance, taxes and fees, delivered in four low-risk iterations.

What I found in the delivery and support data

  • Configuration competes with new capability. A meaningful share of delivery effort went to setup work rather than new features.
  • Setup surfaces late. Tax, fee and rule details tended to arrive during testing instead of discovery.
  • Support absorbs routine changes. A steady share of support requests were configuration updates the business could own.
  • Launch speed is a lever. Most of the time to launch a program is setup, so faster setup means faster time to market.

How I framed it

A phased path that starts with no new platform, a short design study to size the build, and a pilot on a single program so risk stays small and results are measurable.

How a configuration change ships

    Guardrails built into the design

    • VersionedEvery change is tracked, with a diff against production.
    • Maker-checkerOne person edits, a second approves before anything ships.
    • Re-rate previewTest a sample of real policies before publishing.
    • ReversibleRollback is one step; retire never means delete.
    Iteration 1 · start hereConfiguration artifacts

    Guided intake produces a coverage matrix and tax and fee matrix engineering builds from. No new platform.

    Iteration 2Read-only console

    One view of every program's setup, with a diff against production.

    Iteration 3Self-service to test

    Business edits, re-rates a sample of real policies and publishes to test with approvals.

    Iteration 4Release on demand

    Approved changes promoted with audit trail and rollback. Retire never means delete.

    Shows Evidence-based business case · Platform thinking · Phased de-risking · Executive framing

    PRD

    AI-assisted submission intake for commercial trucking underwriting (Phase 1)

    Owner
    Tiffany Woods, Senior PM
    Status
    Draft for Go/No-Go
    Release
    Phase 1 pilot: new-business auto liability and physical damage

    Problem

    Trucking submissions arrive from retail agents as email packages: ACORD applications, vehicle and driver schedules in spreadsheets, scanned loss runs and free-text notes. Intake staff key it all by hand. Missing, stale or conflicting data (a mistyped VIN, a unit count that doesn't match the USDOT record, old loss runs) surfaces late, so underwriters chase agents instead of underwriting, and quotes go out slowly or on bad data.

    Users

    Submission intake staff (primary), underwriters and underwriting managers (review and decisions), retail agents and brokers (submitters), rating and actuarial teams (consumers of clean data).

    Scope

    In

    Extract and normalize submission documents into one structured record. Validate against deterministic checks: VIN check digit and decode, USDOT and operating-authority lookup, address standardization, cross-document consistency. Field-level confidence with a link to the source page. Completeness score and a drafted missing-info request to the agent. Route quote-ready records to the underwriting queue, plus an audit log.

    Out

    Underwriting decisions, pricing, declinations and binding (always human). Ordering MVRs or other consumer reports (regulated; stays manual). Renewals and other lines until Phase 2.

    Success metrics, 90-day pilot

    MetricTargetGuardrail
    Accuracy, rating-critical fields≥ 98%< 97% two weeks running → roll back to assist-only
    Submission to quote-ready time−50% or moreBaseline measured in week 0
    Complete on first underwriter touch70%+No field auto-filled without a source
    Critical data errors reaching a quote0Any one pauses auto-routing
    Underwriter field overrides< 10%> 15% on any field → model and rules review

    How it routes

    1. Intakeemail, portal, ACORD PDFs, spreadsheets, loss runs
    2. Extracteach field with confidence and its source location
    3. ValidateVIN, USDOT, address and cross-document checks. Deterministic, always first
    4. Flaggaps and conflicts with a completeness score. Never guess a missing value
    5. Routequote-ready · assisted review · missing-info request to agent · underwriter
    6. Log & sampleevery field change with its source, plus a 10% QA sample

    Go/No-Go for the pilot

    • All critical eval cases pass
    • Overall pass rate ≥ 95%
    • Underwriting signs off on validation rules
    • Privacy review of driver data handling
    • Rollback to manual intake tested

    24 cases. A third test what the AI must never guess.

    Mark any case as failing to see how the release gate responds. A quote built on a confident wrong VIN or unit count costs more than a slow right one.

    Gate rules: Critical must pass 100% (any failure is No-Go). High ≥ 95%, fix before pilot. Medium and low ≥ 90%, tracked. Every human override becomes a candidate new case.

    $0

    Hours saved = volume × manual intake minutes − (quote-ready × QA sample × 3 min + routed × assisted minutes). Value = hours × loaded cost + data errors avoided × rework time × loaded cost. Not counted: faster quote turnaround and hit-ratio lift, which I’d measure in the pilot rather than promise.
